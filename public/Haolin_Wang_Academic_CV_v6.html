RESEARCH SUMMARY

My research makes pavement condition data trustworthy enough to drive life-cycle maintenance decisions. Over four years at Georgia Tech’s Smart City Infrastructure Lab I have (1) established how to assess and standardize cracking-data quality across state DOTs (NCHRP 01-57B; Transportation Research Record), (2) developed methods that align repeated and multi-sensor surveys so that true deterioration can be measured over time, (3) designed evaluation metrics and open benchmarks for crack-detection models, and (4) connected condition data to treatment decisions and automated maintenance. My dissertation integrates this work into a digital twin of pavement cracking: an end-to-end pipeline that turns millimeter-resolution 3D surveys into persistent crack objects, keeps them synchronized across survey cycles and sensing modalities, propagates measurement uncertainty from data collection to the final condition rating, and uses the resulting state to track deterioration across the pavement life cycle, prioritize treatment timing and location, and plan robotic crack sealing. I pair this with four years of teaching data analytics and AI for civil engineering, currently as Head TA, recognized by Georgia Tech CEE’s Bill Schutz Graduate Teaching Assistant Award.

RESEARCH EXPERIENCE

Graduate Research Assistant, Smart City Infrastructure Lab, Georgia Tech (PI: Dr. James Tsai) Jan 2023 – Present

Projects funded by NCHRP and GDOT. Work is organized below by the three stages of the pavement-cracking digital-twin framework that structures my dissertation (creation, synchronization, and application), plus the evaluation and standardization work that makes the twin trustworthy. Together, these stages connect raw survey data to life-cycle performance monitoring and maintenance decisions.

1. Digital Twin Creation: cracking data quality, uncertainty, and crack-object extraction

Proposed a two-level methodology for assessing cracking-data quality against ground references: an end-to-end coarse-to-fine crack-map comparison plus a decomposed diagnosis separating sensor limitations from detection-algorithm failures; validated across pavement types, sensing systems, and algorithms in Florida, Mississippi, and Texas, giving agencies a known-quality basis for the condition histories used in performance modeling and life-cycle decisions (Transportation Research Record, accepted).

Leading an uncertainty-quantification study that traces how error enters at each step of the crack-measurement pipeline (data collection, crack segmentation, vectorization, and aggregated metrics) and how much of it survives to the final condition rating, using repeat-run 3D laser surveys of US-80 as the empirical benchmark, so that year-to-year condition changes can be distinguished from measurement noise in deterioration modeling (manuscript in preparation).

Maintain and extend the lab’s Crack Vector Model (CVM) library, which converts crack masks and range images into topological node–link crack graphs with per-link width, length, and orientation.

Co-authored a review of deep-learning crack detection (learning paradigms, generalizability, datasets) (arXiv 2025).

Designing structure-aware thin-crack detection that reduces crack fragmentation on 3D range images by predicting crack centerline and width alongside the mask and then decoding continuity across gaps where depth evidence is weak; the study tests image rendering, topology-aware losses, and orientation-guided decoding as sources of improvement (early stage).

2. Digital-Physical Synchronization: multi-temporal and cross-modal data alignment and calibration

Co-developed a feature-based registration method aligning repeated 3D pavement surveys so true deterioration can be separated from image-offset artifacts, enabling crack-level deterioration tracking across survey years (Computer-Aided Civil and Infrastructure Engineering, 2025).

Defining and co-developing an unsupervised deep-learning method for deformable multi-temporal crack-map registration in support of crack-propagation monitoring (manuscript in preparation).

Developing and validating a pipeline for using low-cost RGB (dashcam) imagery as a surrogate for high-resolution 3D laser surveys, enabling more frequent, cost-effective monitoring between survey cycles and a finer temporal resolution for life-cycle condition histories: calibrating how crack information degrades from laser to RGB on co-registered pairs, testing whether the surrogate preserves the crack measures agencies act on, and triggering high-resolution updates only where change is detected (manuscript in preparation).

3. Digital Twin Application: crack condition assessment, deterioration tracking, and treatment prescription

Proposed a link-level crack-density indicator on the CVM graph that replaces grid-based density (ASTM E3303) with a buffer around each crack link; calibrated and cross-validated on 150 crack maps spanning four crack types and road classes for alligator-crack classification, providing a consistent severity measure for treatment selection (ASCE Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering, in revision).

Formalized robotic crack-sealing path planning as a sliding-window arc-routing problem (a Rural Postman Problem variant) in which a sealing vehicle advances along the lane while a robotic arm must trace every crack within operating constraints under a full-coverage requirement, lowering the cost of preventive maintenance that extends pavement service life; benchmarking solvers (manuscript in preparation).

4. Auxiliary toolbox: supporting methods for the pipeline

Lane-marking detection for wheelpath-based distress measurement: built OrthoLaneMark, a 1,391-image presence-aware benchmark on orthographic 3D-laser imagery with a Gated Region Accuracy metric and nine re-implemented detectors across four mechanisms; established that wheelpath-region accuracy is bounded by presence reasoning rather than sub-pixel localization (journal submission, 2026; TRB 2027 presentation).

Designed the CRAck Structural Difference Index (CRASDI), a modular, crack-topology-aware index that compares crack maps as graphs and decomposes their difference into geometry, length, width, and orientation; benchmarked against six conventional metrics on 1,405 prediction–reference pairs across nine public datasets with dataset-wise Spearman rank correlation, and diagnosed atypical rankings caused by annotation irregularities, thin crack traces, and repeated prediction failures (CACAIE, under review). Released the open-source Python package and reproducibility scripts (github.com/THiNK327/CRASDI).

5. Standardization and framework development

Core member of NCHRP Project 01-57B (Validating Standard Definitions for Comparable Pavement Cracking Data): developed and validated quality-assessment and crack-definition protocols, contributed to the Final Report, Tech Brief, proposed revisions to AASHTO R 85, and crack-data aggregation standard; co-authored responses to the panel ballot and presented to the panel and at RPUG 2025 and 2026.

Conducting a systematic review of pavement digital twins for life-cycle maintenance and rehabilitation decision-making, framing creation, synchronization, and application as a bidirectional twin loop; presented the initial framework at the ACM SIGSPATIAL Workshop on Spatial Digital Twins (2025).
